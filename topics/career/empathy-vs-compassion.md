# Empathy vs. compassion

A colleague struggling with a task may need understanding, practical help, and clear expectations.

**Empathy** involves understanding another person's perspective or responding to their emotions. **Compassion** involves concern for someone who is struggling and a desire to help.

## Two aspects of empathy

Empathy can include:

- **Perspective-taking:** understanding what the situation looks like to the other person.
- **Emotional empathy:** feeling an emotional response to what the other person is experiencing.

Neither guarantees action. Understanding someone's frustration does not by itself decide what support to offer.

Compassion adds motivation to help. It can lead to listening, removing an obstacle, or giving information the person needs.

```text
Empathy
"Understand what this is like for them"
                  |
          can inform a response
                  |
Compassion
"Care about the difficulty and seek to help"
```

Someone can help a frustrated colleague without feeling frustrated themselves.

## Apply both to a work problem

Suppose a teammate repeatedly misses a delivery date.

An empathetic response investigates the situation. Perhaps the scope keeps changing, a dependency is unavailable, or the estimate was unrealistic.

A compassionate response uses that understanding to offer useful help:

- Clarify the remaining work.
- Remove or escalate a dependency.
- Reduce scope when appropriate.
- Agree on a realistic plan.

The missed commitment still needs to be discussed. Understanding the difficulty makes that conversation better informed.

## Why avoiding the issue can be unhelpful

Kim Scott's Radical Candor framework uses "Ruinous Empathy" for caring about someone while avoiding a needed direct challenge.

This label describes a feedback pattern. It does not mean empathy itself is harmful.

Saying "Everything is fine" when the missed dates are affecting others leaves the teammate without an accurate picture.

A clearer response might be:

> The last two deliveries missed their agreed dates, which delayed integration. What changed, and how can the next plan account for it?

## Combine support with an expectation

```text
Understand the difficulty
          |
state the observed impact
          |
agree on help and expectations
          |
follow up on the result
```

Support should match the problem and the available capacity. Helping may mean involving another owner rather than taking over the work.

Care remains useful when it includes both the person's circumstances and the consequences that need attention.
