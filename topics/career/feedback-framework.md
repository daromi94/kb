# Feedback framework

Feedback helps someone understand what they did well and what they can improve.

Kim Scott's Radical Candor framework looks at two dimensions:

- **Care personally:** show concern for the person.
- **Challenge directly:** communicate the important point clearly.

The combination describes how feedback lands in a particular conversation.

## The four quadrants

```text
            Care personally
                 ^
                 |
Ruinous          |       Radical
Empathy          |       Candor
                 |
-----------------+------------------> Challenge
                 |                    directly
Manipulative     |       Obnoxious
Insincerity      |       Aggression
                 |
```

| Pattern                  | Care    | Direct challenge |
| ------------------------ | ------- | ---------------- |
| Radical Candor           | Present | Present          |
| Ruinous Empathy          | Present | Missing          |
| Obnoxious Aggression     | Missing | Present          |
| Manipulative Insincerity | Missing | Missing          |

In practical terms:

- **Radical Candor:** explain the issue while treating the person with respect.
- **Ruinous Empathy:** avoid a needed point to spare discomfort.
- **Obnoxious Aggression:** deliver criticism without showing care.
- **Manipulative Insincerity:** avoid an honest, caring exchange, perhaps through empty praise or talking behind someone's back.

The labels describe behavior, rather than permanent personality types.

## Build a specific conversation

Suppose a release plan lacks a rollback procedure.

Compare three responses:

- "Looks good." The missing information stays hidden.
- "This is careless." The response attacks the person without explaining the gap.
- "The rollback steps are missing. If the release fails, the team needs a tested way to recover. Can those steps be added before rollout?"

The third response identifies the behavior, its consequence, and the needed change. It gives the author something concrete to address.

## Leave room for missing context

Feedback is an observation from a point of view. The reviewer may have missed a separate rollback document or misunderstood the plan.

Ask a question and listen to the answer:

> Is the rollback procedure documented elsewhere, or does it still need to be designed?

Being direct about the concern does not require certainty about the cause or motive.

## Include useful praise

Praise also benefits from detail:

> The rollout included a tested rollback procedure, so the team could recover quickly when the new version failed.

This tells the person which behavior mattered and why it is worth repeating.

## Check how the message landed

A good intention does not guarantee that feedback is clear or respectful.

After a difficult point, check what the other person understood. Clarify a softened message if the issue remains unclear. If the delivery was hurtful, address it while keeping the underlying concern explicit.

Feedback should also flow in both directions. Ask for a specific improvement and respond constructively when someone offers it.
