# Argument versus quarrel

An argument and a quarrel can look identical from the outside, yet
they differ on two axes: their target and their goal. An argument
targets an idea to find the truth. A quarrel targets a person to win
status.

## Target: idea or person

An argument targets the claim. You examine its structure and
challenge a specific part: the premise is false, or the conclusion
does not follow. You attack a piece of the reasoning, never the
reasoner.

A quarrel abandons the reasoning and attacks the speaker. This is the
ad hominem fallacy — Latin for "to the person." Instead of proving
the idea wrong, you try to prove the person foolish, incompetent, or
malicious.

## Goal: truth or status

An argument is positive-sum. Both sides work on the same problem, so
a flaw one side exposes is one both are now rid of. Finding the fatal
flaw in your own reasoning is not a loss — you have eliminated a bad
idea and moved closer to the truth.

A quarrel is zero-sum. The goal is not to solve a problem but to gain
status, which only transfers: for one person to win, the other must
lose face.

## Trust sustains hard disagreement

Blunt, sustained disagreement is possible only when both sides trust
that the exchange is an argument over the truth, not a quarrel aimed
at the person. When someone finds a deep flaw in your position, you
can take it impersonally because you trust the target is the idea,
not you.
