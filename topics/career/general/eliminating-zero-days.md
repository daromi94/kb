# Eliminating zero days

A zero day is a workday where, despite being busy, you accomplish
nothing of actual value. This happens because urgent work (fires,
notifications, deadlines) consumes all available energy. When the
fires die down, the brain defaults to low-value tasks — refreshing
email, quick syncs — instead of switching to important but
non-urgent work like strategic planning or skill-building.

## The "one day" list

The fix is removing choice overload from downtime. Maintain a
running list of important-but-not-urgent tasks so you never have to
decide what to do when a free hour appears.

**Capture in the chaos.** While fighting urgent fires, you will
think of things you would do "if you only had the time." Write them
down immediately.

**Capitalize on lulls.** When a meeting cancels or quiet time
appears, open the list, pick an item, and start. Do not refresh
Slack.

## The litmus test

After completing a task from the list, ask: "What do I have to show
for it?" The answer must be one of:

| Outcome             | Example                                      |
|---------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Learned something   | Deep dive into epoll or io_uring             |
| Created an artifact | Working script, POC, or documentation        |
| Made real progress  | Path to a major goal is demonstrably shorter |

If the answer is none of these, the task was busywork.
