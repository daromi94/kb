# Force multiplication

Individual excellence creates linear career growth. Amplifying the
capabilities of your teammates creates exponential returns —
teammates grow, you gain a reputation as a leader, and the
organization benefits from increased output.

## Teach through questions

Treat time spent teaching as an investment, not a cost. Instead of
giving teammates the answer, ask guiding questions that help them
reframe the problem and learn to solve it themselves.

## Identify hidden potential

Look for teammates who ask insightful questions or show intrinsic
curiosity. Help them build confidence by giving progressively larger
opportunities to present their ideas and lead.

## Share your mental models

Show people how you think, not just what to do. Narrated work
sessions — recording your screen while writing a design document,
verbalizing your thought process, false starts, and decision logic —
make your expertise transferable.

## Create visibility for others

Advocate for teammates when they are not in the room. Highlight
their specific contributions to leadership and create opportunities
for them to showcase their work to a broader audience.

## Delegate to stretch

True delegation is not load balancing or micromanaging. Assign
challenging work with clear success criteria and give the person
space to struggle, figure it out, and course-correct on their own.

## Make feedback safe

Deliver critical feedback privately. State that you share it because
you care about their growth. Skip the feedback sandwich — focus on
specific observations, the impact of the behavior, and a clear
actionable alternative.
