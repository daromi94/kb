# Navigating ambiguity

Real-world engineering is full of vague directives: "investigate
this," "make it ready for scale," "improve reliability." The
instinct is to start building immediately, but building a solution
to a poorly defined problem wastes months of effort. The goal is not
to find a solution — it is to define the problem space. Once
boundaries, constraints, and success metrics are clear, the
technical path forward becomes obvious.

## The question dump

Spend 10-15 minutes writing down every question you have about the
vague directive. What does success look like? What are the
constraints? Who are the stakeholders? Do not filter — just capture.

## Organize by power

Triage the list into three groups: answers you already know, answers
you can easily find, and major unknowns. Prioritize questions that
eliminate entire areas of unnecessary investigation.

## Seek answers systematically

Take priority questions to the right stakeholders. Do your own
research first. Ask each person you interview who you should talk to
next.

## Iterate and refine

New questions will emerge as you get answers. This is healthy
progress, not scope creep. Add them to the list and keep digging.

## Document and share

Compile your questions, discovered answers, and — crucially — the
things you still do not know. This document becomes a map that
brings stakeholders along and transforms a blank page into a clear
roadmap. A vague directive like "improve reliability" becomes
"reduce customer-impacting incidents by 80% within six months while
maintaining current performance metrics."
