# Effective one-on-ones

A one-on-one is a regular conversation between a manager and a team member. It creates space for problems, feedback, and decisions that may not fit in a project meeting.

The meeting is useful when it helps both people understand the work and agree on needed support.

## Keep routine status easy to read

A short written update can cover completed work, next steps, and known risks before the meeting.

This leaves more time for questions that need discussion:

- Which priority should change?
- What is blocking progress?
- Where is feedback needed?
- Which opportunity would help develop a skill?

Status can still belong in the conversation when its meaning or consequences are unclear.

```text
Written update
      |
shared view of current work
      |
one-on-one: discuss decisions and support
      |
agreed actions and owners
```

## Bring three kinds of topics

### Current work

Discuss obstacles and tradeoffs rather than reciting every task.

For example:

> The migration and incident follow-up need the same engineer this week. Which outcome should take priority?

Give the manager enough context to help with the decision.

### Growth

Make development concrete:

- Identify a skill to practice.
- Choose a real task that uses it.
- Agree on support and feedback.
- Review what happened afterward.

"Improve technical leadership" might become "lead the next design review, with feedback afterward on how alternatives were compared."

This makes progress easier to discuss throughout the year, rather than only during a performance review.

### Future direction

Ask about upcoming changes that affect the work:

- Which team goals are changing?
- Which capability may become more important?
- Which dependency needs attention before the next project?
- What context can be shared about a decision?

The manager may not know the answer or be able to share every detail. Clarify what can be decided now and what needs follow-up.

## Invite specific feedback

Broad questions often produce broad answers. Ask about an observable situation:

> In the design review, did the explanation make the tradeoffs clear? Which part was hard to follow?

Also share feedback on the support needed from the manager. A useful meeting allows both people to identify improvements.

## End with a small agreement

Record the action, owner, and expected timing:

> The engineer will prepare two scope options by Tuesday. The manager will confirm which deadline is flexible.

Not every conversation needs an action item. When a decision is made, recording it prevents both people from leaving with different expectations.
