# Philip Su on scaling your impact

Philip Su (Distinguished Engineer at Meta, early OpenAI employee) on
growing beyond individual code output. Every engineer eventually hits
a ceiling where personal output maxes out. Reaching distinguished
levels requires shifting focus to helping the team choose the right
things to work on.

## Ownership thinking

Most developers think like hired hands, not business owners. The
distinguishing question is: "If it were my money and my company,
would I spend my time working on this?" Engineers who think like
owners naturally gravitate toward high-leverage work and away from
busywork.

## Earning trust through small things

Trust is built incrementally. If you cannot be trusted with mundane
tasks, no one will trust you with large features. When your current
job is fixing bugs, become the fastest and best bug fixer on the team
before demanding more scope.

## Always be building

Do not wait for permission or a paycheck to start building. Build
first, showcase your work, and opportunities follow. Side projects
demonstrate initiative and create leverage for career advancement.

## First-principles thinking

Push the boundaries of what seems possible. Even when resources are
constrained, explore the absolute limits of the solution space before
settling on a scoped approach. This prevents premature narrowing.

## Direct feedback

Lean into difficult conversations. Give feedback directly and attach
your name to it rather than hiding behind anonymity. Honest,
attributed feedback builds trust and drives better outcomes.
