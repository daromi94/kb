# Feedback framework

Radical Candor maps feedback behavior along two axes: Care Personally
and Challenge Directly. The combination produces four quadrants.

```text
          Care Personally
                ^
                |
  Ruinous       |  Radical
  Empathy       |  Candor
                |
  --------------|--------------> Challenge Directly
                |
  Manipulative  |  Obnoxious
  Insincerity   |  Aggression
                |
```

## The four quadrants

| Quadrant                 | Care | Challenge | Effect                          |
|--------------------------|------|-----------|---------------------------------|
| Radical Candor           | High | High      | Growth through honest feedback  |
| Obnoxious Aggression     | Low  | High      | Truth told without empathy      |
| Ruinous Empathy          | High | Low       | Kindness that prevents growth   |
| Manipulative Insincerity | Low  | Low       | Passive-aggressive or political |

**Radical Candor** is the goal. Challenge someone directly because you
care about their growth. Clear, honest feedback delivered with genuine
concern.

**Obnoxious Aggression** gets the truth out but without showing you
care. The feedback may be accurate, but the lack of empathy makes it
hard to hear.

**Ruinous Empathy** is the most common failure mode. Avoiding hard
feedback to spare feelings prevents the person from improving.

**Manipulative Insincerity** is the worst quadrant. Neither caring nor
challenging — feedback is withheld, political, or shared behind the
person's back.

## Key principles

**Compass, not a label.** The framework describes behavior in a
specific moment, not a fixed personality type. Everyone moves between
quadrants. The goal is to spend more time in Radical Candor, not to
achieve it permanently.

**Order of operations.** When in doubt, Obnoxious Aggression is better
than Ruinous Empathy. Telling the truth without enough care is still
more useful than withholding feedback out of false kindness. The aim is
always to move up and to the right — but getting the truth out matters
most.

**Humble feedback.** Radical Candor is bidirectional. It requires being
open to challenge, not just delivering it. Willingness to admit you are
wrong is a prerequisite for earning the trust that makes direct feedback
possible.
