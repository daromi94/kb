# Leadership review cadence

A culture of reliability has to be driven from the top. Senior
leaders must spend their own bandwidth understanding why systems
operate the way they do, not just reading summary metrics.

## Mandatory and recurring

At AWS, a mandatory weekly meeting where senior leaders and engineers
cross-examine correction of errors (COE) documents is cited as a core
causal factor in the company's reliability record. The cadence is the
point: reliability review is not an exception triggered by a big
outage, it is a standing obligation.

## Cross-examination, not approval

The forum is not a rubber stamp. Leaders ask the uncomfortable
questions that a team writing its own COE may avoid — about missed
signals, process gaps, and recurring themes across incidents. This
pressure keeps COEs honest and prevents them from softening into
exercises in self-justification.

## Why it keeps the organization grounded

When leaders engage deeply with COEs, the entire organization stays
grounded in the real architecture rather than a sanitized version of
it. Leaders who only see dashboards lose the concrete detail that
makes reliability decisions correct. Without this engagement,
postmortem culture quietly drifts toward blame-avoidance and shallow
root causes.
