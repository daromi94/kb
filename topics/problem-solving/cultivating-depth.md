# Cultivating depth

Practices that build lasting understanding of a problem domain rather
than surface-level familiarity with a solution. Apply these alongside
active problem-solving to deepen intuition over time.

## Deconstruct the magic

When a technique or solution works, but you do not fully understand
why, investigate the underlying mechanics. Identify the specific
principle that makes it work rather than accepting the result at face
value.

## Build from partial progress

Focus on intermediate handholds. Instead of restarting from scratch
after failure, adapt and improve prototype strategies based on what
partially succeeds. What works reveals constraints; what fails
eliminates dead ends. Both narrow the search space.

## Force consolidation through writing

Document newly learned concepts by reconstructing the arguments and
steps required to reach a conclusion. Writing exposes gaps in
understanding and prevents knowledge degradation.

## Embrace serendipitous learning

Avoid over-optimizing the learning process. Goal-directed research
has blind spots. Unstructured exploration — browsing physical
resources, casual interactions outside targeted objectives —
introduces adjacent concepts that deliberate searches miss.
