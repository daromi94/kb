# Four-phase method

A four-step process for solving problems: understand, plan, execute,
review.

## 1. Understanding the problem

You cannot solve what you do not define. Restate the problem in your
own words — if you cannot state it fluently, you do not understand
it yet.

Identify three things:

- **Unknown:** What are you trying to find or prove?
- **Data:** What information is available?
- **Condition:** Which rules or constraints link the data to the unknown?

Write the parts down separately. Draw a figure or introduce notation
that makes the constraints visible.

Then probe the condition:

- Is it satisfiable?
- Is it sufficient to pin down the unknown?
- Is any part redundant or contradictory?

## 2. Devising a plan

Find the connection between the data and the unknown. If no direct
connection is visible, try one of these moves:

- **Recall.** Have you seen this problem or its unknown before? Is
  there a solved problem whose method or result you could reuse?
- **Restate.** Try a different formulation. Go back to definitions.
- **Solve a related problem.** A more general, more specific, or
  analogous one. Could you solve a part of it?
- **Relax a constraint.** Drop part of the condition. How is the
  unknown determined now? How can it vary?
- **Work forward from the data.** Derive something useful from what
  you have, narrowing the gap to the unknown.
- **Check coverage.** Did you use all the data? The whole condition?
  Every essential concept?

## 3. Carrying out the plan

Execute with patience and precision. At each step ask: can you see
that it is correct, and can you prove it?

## 4. Looking back

Examine the result and the path that led to it. This strengthens
your ability to solve future problems.

- **Verify the result.** Can you check the argument? Can you derive
  the result by a different method?
- **Generalize.** Can this method or result be applied to other
  problems?

Your conception of the problem is incomplete when you start. It
shifts as you make progress, and again as you near the solution —
expect to revisit earlier phases.
