# Socratic method

Stress-test ideas through dialogue. Instead of accepting a claim,
subject it to a sequence of questions that expose hidden assumptions,
missing evidence, and unexamined consequences. What survives is
stronger than what was first proposed.

## The arc

1. **Claim.** State the premise or proposed solution as a fact.
2. **Elenchus.** Cross-examine. Probe for contradictions, exceptions,
   and missing evidence.
3. **Aporia.** The claim breaks down. What looked complete reveals
   gaps, conflations, or unverified leaps.
4. **Maieutics.** Build a refined hypothesis from what survived —
   testable and grounded in evidence rather than intuition.

## Categories of questions

Each category opens a distinct angle of attack.

- **Clarification.** What exactly does each term mean?
- **Assumptions.** What must also be true for this to hold?
- **Evidence.** What data supports this? How do we know?
- **Alternatives.** What would someone with different constraints
  choose?
- **Consequences.** What are the second-order effects? What if it
  fails?
